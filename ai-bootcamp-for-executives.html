AI bootcamp · Executives

An AI bootcamp for executives: build one real workflow before you bet the budget.

Most executive AI programs are lectures and case studies. They are good for vocabulary. They rarely prepare a leader for the questions that arrive the following Monday. Which of our workflows is worth automating first? What will it cost to run? Can our people maintain it, or are we signing up for a consultant forever? What data are we allowed to send to a model?

The fastest way to calibrated answers is to build one workflow yourself, on your own business, with an experienced builder next to you. Below is what that kind of program should cover and how to choose one.

How to choose

How to choose an AI program as an executive.

  1. Will you touch the tools?If the agenda is all talks and panels, you will leave with opinions and no calibration. One afternoon of building teaches more about cost and risk than a week of slides.
  2. Is the material your business or someone else’s?Case studies about large companies are interesting. A workflow from your own operation, built in the room, is what you can act on.
  3. Is governance specific?Ask how the program handles proprietary data. A real answer names tools, retention settings and review steps.
  4. Does your team come too?A leader who learns alone comes back to a team that has not. Programs that train the leadership group together move faster afterward.
  5. What is left behind?Look for documentation, a maintenance guide and a prioritized list of what to build next, so the work continues after the trainers leave.

When university executive education is the better pick: if you want a recognized credential, a peer network from large companies, or board-level strategy framing, a business school program will serve you better. We are the option for leaders who want to build.
