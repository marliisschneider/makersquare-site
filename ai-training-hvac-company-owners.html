What you do today.
What you can do after training.

We read general manager postings from HVAC and home service companies and listened to owners describe their week. The same six parts of the job came up every time. Each one sits next to what you can do once you’ve built your own AI tools with us.

Your jobAfter MakerSquare training
Your job
Keep the phones answered and the board full

You make sure every call gets booked, reshuffle the dispatch board when a no-heat call comes in, and lose sleep over the calls that go to voicemail after hours.

After MakerSquare training

Text back every missed call within a minute

When a call goes unanswered, your tool texts the customer, asks for the address, the system’s age, and what’s wrong, and leaves a ready-to-book ticket for your CSR to confirm first thing in the morning.

Your job
Price the work and send estimates

You keep the price book current as supplier prices go up, build good, better, and best options on replacements, and decide which jobs are worth taking.

After MakerSquare training

Send the estimate the same day, with the margin checked

Your tech’s notes and photos become good, better, and best options from your own price book. Before anything goes out, it adds up equipment, labor, and overhead and flags any option below your margin floor.

Your job
Follow up and fill the slow months

You chase open estimates, recall maintenance members twice a year, sell new agreements, and try to keep your techs busy in April and October.

After MakerSquare training

Fill spring and fall without making the calls yourself

Every week it pulls members due for a tune-up, estimates nobody followed up on, and customers with systems over 12 years old, then drafts a personal text or email for each one with open dates. You approve what goes out.

Your job
Know your numbers

You watch revenue, gross margin, average ticket, close rate, callbacks, and cash, usually from reports that show up weeks after the fact.

After MakerSquare training

Read your numbers in plain English every Monday

It pulls last week from QuickBooks and your field service software: close rate by tech, average ticket, callbacks, invoices past 30 days, and any cash gap coming up. Then it writes a one-page summary of what changed and what to look at.

Your job
Hire, train, and keep your team

You hire techs and CSRs in a tight labor market, train them on how your shop does things, and check the quality of every install.

After MakerSquare training

Get a new CSR ready in two weeks

From your own call scripts and policies, it builds a day-by-day onboarding plan with short quizzes. Your team also gets an assistant that answers questions about your pricing, membership rules, and install standards, so fewer of those questions come to you.

Your job
Win reviews and local leads

You manage your Google Business Profile, ask for reviews, answer the bad ones, and decide where the marketing money goes.

After MakerSquare training

Turn every finished job into a review and a post

When a job closes, it sends the review request. It drafts a reply to every new review and a weekly post with job photos from your service area for you to approve. Each month it shows which lead sources turned into booked jobs.

Build or buy?

Keep your software.
Build what it doesn’t do.

Your field service software already sells AI add-ons, and some are worth buying. Learning to build gets you the rest.

Your software stays

It runs dispatch. You build around it.

ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber keeps handling dispatch and invoicing. You build the pieces it doesn’t have: the Monday numbers report, follow-up rules that match how your shop sells, an assistant that knows your install standards.

You own it

No add-on fee for every seat

What you build runs on your own accounts. You pay for what the tools use, and when you want a rule changed, you change it that afternoon instead of filing a feature request.

Buy smarter

Tell the real features from the demos

Once you’ve built an agent yourself, you know what it takes. The next time a vendor pitches an AI add-on, you’ll know whether it’s worth the monthly bill.

How you learn it

You build on your real business

You describe what you want in plain English, and Claude Code writes the code. We teach you how to check it, fix it when it breaks, and deploy it. Questions

AI training for HVAC owners, answered

How can an HVAC owner learn AI?

Build something for your own shop. A tool that follows up on open estimates or writes your Monday numbers report teaches you how AI works and keeps paying off after training. All four of our formats work this way: weekend workshop, 2-week immersive, private team training, and 1:1.

Why build AI tools instead of buying them?

Keep the software you already pay for. Build the pieces it doesn't do, like a Monday numbers report or follow-up rules that match how your shop works. You own what you build, you pay for what it uses instead of another monthly add-on, and you learn enough to tell which vendor AI features are worth buying.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Most of our students have never written code. You describe what you want in plain English, and we teach you to check, fix, and deploy what Claude Code writes.

Will it work with ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber?

Usually. These platforms offer API access on some plans, and QuickBooks, Gmail, and Google Sheets connect easily. Tell us what you run on the intro call and we'll check what your tools can connect to.

Can my office manager and CSRs train too?

Yes. Private team training is for groups of 3 to 15 and runs at your shop, built around your software. We quote it after the intro call.

Is it online?

The weekend workshop and the 2-week immersive are in person in Austin. 1:1 training can be in person or online. Private team training happens at your shop.

What does it cost?

The 2-week immersive is $3,999 and the weekend workshop is $1,399. Team and 1:1 training are quoted after the intro call.

Tell us about your shop.

On the intro call we look at your business and your team, then suggest the workshop, the immersive, team training, or 1:1. If none of them fit, we’ll tell you.
