Building With AI vs. Using AI: What's the Difference?

Marliis Schneider July 8, 2026 4 min read
Key takeaways
  • Using AI: you open a chat (maybe a Claude Project or a custom GPT with your files loaded) and ask each time. Building with AI: you make a tool that starts on its own, works inside your real tools, and does the task every time it comes up.
  • You don't need to know how to code. You need to know your task well enough to explain the steps to a new hire.
  • Start with something you do every week, the same way each time. That's where a build pays you back.

Most people we meet use AI the same way. They open ChatGPT or Claude, paste something in, copy the answer out, and repeat that tomorrow. You may also use Projects in Claude, or Projects and custom GPTs in ChatGPT, so it already knows your files, your instructions and how you like things written. That's using AI, and it helps a lot. You write faster and research faster.

Building with AI goes a step further. Even with a good Project set up, you still have to open the chat, ask, and move the answer to wherever it goes. A build does those steps for you. It starts on its own (a new lead, a new email, every Monday at 8 AM), works with your real tools like Gmail, Outlook or your CRM, and puts the result where it belongs. You set it up once and it keeps running.

What using AI looks like

At a minimum, using AI looks like this. Say you're in sales. A new lead comes in. You open ChatGPT or Claude and paste in their LinkedIn, your notes, and your directions: what to say, how long to make it, how you like to sound. It writes a follow-up email. You fix the parts it got wrong, copy it into Gmail, and send it. Then the next lead comes in and you paste all of that in again.

Or maybe you're further along and use it more efficiently. You've set up a Claude Project or a custom GPT, so your directions and example emails are already saved, and you don't have to paste your instructions every time. But you're still the one doing the work, one lead at a time. You notice the lead came in, open the chat, paste in their info, check the email, and copy it into Gmail. Next week you save the same 20 minutes on the same kind of email.

What building with AI looks like

Now say you build a tool instead. Here's what happens when a new lead emails you:

  1. The tool reads the email to see what the person wants, and looks up who they are.
  2. It follows your team's rules for that kind of request. Every team has them, written down or not: a pricing question gets one kind of answer, a demo request gets another.
  3. It writes the reply in your voice, because it learned from emails you've already sent.
  4. It saves the reply in your Gmail drafts.

You open Gmail, read the draft, change anything you want, and hit send. You never opened a chat. You set it up once, and it does this for every lead.

People are already building tools like this for their own work:

  • Evelyn at Credix (customer support): her weekly support report took hours. Now every Sunday her tool pulls the data, works out the numbers, writes the report and posts a summary in Slack. She spends a few minutes reviewing it and adding her take.
  • Cameron Dowd (SaaS account executive): researching each company before a cold email took hours a week. Now he adds a target account, his tool researches the company, and a personal email waits in his Gmail drafts for him to read and send.
  • OpusClip's revenue team (10 people, none had written production code): they used to review 5–10% of sales calls by hand. Their tool now reviews every call and preps renewal briefs automatically.
  • Jennifer Francis (construction project manager): every RFI and submittal email meant four manual steps. Her Outlook agent now downloads the file, renames it to her naming convention, saves it to the right folder, and logs it in the right project hub for her to confirm.
  • Jared Sires (account executive at Anthropic): he was answering customer emails until 9 or 10 PM every night. He built a tool inside Gmail that drafts replies in his voice, using the latest product details. It saves him 10–15 hours a week, and most of Anthropic's sales team now uses his tools.
  • Ted Kilgore (job seeker): he wanted to aim his search instead of applying everywhere. He enters his resume, a target role and a location, and his tool builds a shortlist of 30 companies that fit his background, each with a link to a real hiring signal.
  • An account manager at ABC Legal: had never automated anything. In about an hour, they built an agent that pulls a customer's records and delivers them every day, a chore they used to do by hand.
  • Chris Cox (Hesed House, a homeless shelter): updates about shelter guests arrive by email. His agent reads each one, pulls out a record, and matches it to the right guest on a dashboard for case managers. When it isn't sure, it flags the record for a person to check.

Each one started as a task the person was already doing by hand every week. You can see more builds like these on our student projects page.

Do you need to code?

No. Our students build with Claude Code: you describe what you want in plain English, and it writes and runs the code. The hard part is knowing your own task well enough to explain it. What goes in, what comes out, and what a good result looks like. If you can train a new hire on it, you can describe it to Claude.

Which tasks are worth building?

Ask yourself two questions about a task:

  1. Do I do it every week (or every day)?
  2. Could I explain the steps to a new hire in one or a couple of detailed emails?

Two yeses and it's a good first build. For one-offs, like a tricky email or a quick summary of a long doc, keep using AI in a chat. Build for the work that keeps coming back.

Where to start

Pick one task from your week that passes the test. Write the steps down like you're training someone. Then open Claude Code and ask it to build step 1 only. Get that working, then add step 2. That's how Jennifer built hers: read the email, then grab the file, then rename it, one piece at a time.

Frequently asked questions
What is the difference between using AI and building with AI?
Using AI means you ask ChatGPT or Claude for help each time you need something, even if a Claude Project or custom GPT already knows your files and instructions. Building with AI means you make a tool that handles a recurring task on its own, inside your real tools, like drafting an email in Gmail for every new lead or filing documents from your Outlook inbox.
Do you need to know how to code to build with AI?
No. Tools like Claude Code write and run the code from plain-English instructions. What you need is a clear picture of the task: what goes in, what comes out, and what a good result looks like.
What can a beginner build with AI?
MakerSquare students have built a prospecting agent that drafts sales emails, a tool that shortlists best-fit companies from a resume, an Outlook agent that files construction RFIs and submittals, and a shelter dashboard that turns emails into guest records. Start with a task you do every week, the same way each time.
